Ada Lovelace

1815 — 1852 · Mathematician, first programmer

Ada Lovelace was an English mathematician who, in 1843, translated an Italian article about Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine and then buried the translation under notes three times longer than the original. Those notes contained something nobody had written before: a step-by-step method for the machine to compute Bernoulli numbers — the first published algorithm written for a machine.

More remarkably, she saw past arithmetic. She argued that if a machine could manipulate symbols, it could compose music, handle letters, and produce anything expressible in symbols. That leap — from calculator to general-purpose computer — is why her name sits on the field of computing today.

She died at thirty-six, her notes largely forgotten until the 1950s. The programming language Ada, the Ada Lovelace Medal, and Ada Lovelace Day all now carry her name.
